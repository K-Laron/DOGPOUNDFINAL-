# CHAPTER II: REVIEW OF RELATED LITERATURE AND STUDIES

This chapter presents a critical review of existing literature and studies relevant to the development of a web-based animal shelter management system. It integrates findings on shelter data management, legal frameworks, and technological innovations to provide a theoretical foundation for the Catarman Dog Pound & Animal Shelter Management System.

## Related Literature

### Foreign Literature

The integration of technology into animal welfare is a growing field of interest globally. Jukan, Masip-Bruin, and Amla (2016) explored the application of "Smart Computing" and sensor technologies in animal welfare. Their systematic review highlights how real-time monitoring and data analytics can enhance the efficiency of welfare monitoring in shelters. While their work focuses heavily on IoT and sensors, the underlying principle is highly relevant to this project: centralized digital systems are superior to manual methods for tracking animal health and behavior. This literature supports the proposed system’s Health Module, which aims to digitize the tracking of vaccination and treatment records.

### Local Literature

In the Philippine context, the management of stray animals is heavily governed by legislation. Respicio (2024) provides a comprehensive legal analysis of the responsibilities of Local Government Units (LGUs) under the Animal Welfare Act (RA 8485) and the Anti-Rabies Act (RA 9482). The literature emphasizes that while the laws are robust, implementation remains inconsistent due to a lack of standard protocols and infrastructure in rural municipalities. This directly highlights the gap that the Catarman Dog Pound & Animal Shelter Management System aims to fill—providing the technological infrastructure to help the LGU comply with its legal mandate to maintain a registered, humane, and well-documented pound.

Additionally, Serra (2015) discusses the importance of "Therapeutic Landscape Design" for rehabilitation centers. While focused on physical architecture, this literature underscores the holistic needs of impounded animals. It supports the system's objective to not just "store data," but to actively assist in managing an environment that promotes animal recovery and eventual adoption.

## Related Studies

### Foreign Studies

Several international studies emphasize the critical role of data accuracy in shelter operations. Jagtap (2025) developed "Shelter Soul," a modern web-based platform using the MERN stack designed to bridge the gap between shelters and adopters. This study is particularly relevant as it mirrors the core objectives of the proposed Catarman system—specifically the integration of adoption matching, volunteer coordination, and analytics—confirming that web-based platforms are the current standard for modernizing shelter operations.

The importance of standardized data entry is further supported by Scott, Jones, and Ramirez (2023), who investigated data quality in US shelters. They found that inconsistent records lead to poor decision-making and compromised animal welfare. Similarly, Ly and Protopopova (2023) analyzed intake information and found significant discrepancies between verbal reports and written forms. These studies justify the need for the proposed system's Intake Module, which utilizes required fields and standardized drop-down menus to minimize human error and ensure accurate historical records for every animal.

### Local Studies

In the Philippines, local researchers have begun developing digital solutions for animal welfare, though most focus on specific niches rather than holistic management. Torres et al. (2024) developed PAWrfect Match, a web-based system that uses content-based filtering algorithms to recommend pets to adopters. While highly effective for adoption, their system focuses primarily on the "matchmaking" aspect. The Catarman Dog Pound & Animal Shelter Management System builds upon this by not only facilitating adoption but also managing the internal operations (inventory, medical, intake) that precede adoption.

Mobile solutions have also been explored. Galamiton et al. (2022) developed a mobile-based pet adoption app to reduce shelter overcrowding, finding high user acceptance for its accessibility. Fernandez et al. (2021) created Pawster Care, an Android app combining cruelty reporting with adoption features. While their scope included reporting, the study is significant as it validates the high user acceptance of digital animal welfare initiatives. The proposed Catarman system focuses on the adoption and management aspects, ensuring that the LGU has a single, comprehensive command center for all shelter operations.

## Synthesis of the Art

The reviewed literature and studies collectively establish that manual methods are insufficient for modern animal welfare management. While foreign studies (Jagtap, 2025; Scott et al., 2023) provide the technical and operational blueprint for web-based management and data integrity, local studies (Torres et al., 2024; Fernandez et al., 2021) confirm the Filipino public's readiness for digital adoption platforms. The Catarman Dog Pound & Animal Shelter Management System uniquely bridges the gap identified by Respicio (2024) by providing a localized, all-in-one solution that addresses the specific compliance, inventory, and operational needs of a Philippine LGU, which existing local apps do not fully cover.
